Ships

Hamilton is a working port. Lakers and ocean-going freighters move steel, ore, grain and salt through the harbour year-round, and most of them reach us by way of the Welland Canal and the St. Lawrence Seaway.

This board is a beta. It listens to AIS — the position beacon every sizeable vessel broadcasts continuously on VHF — and lists what was heard over western Lake Ontario, nearest the harbour first.

How to read it

This is what we heard, not what is there. AIS reception here is terrestrial and crowd-sourced, so a vessel out of receiver range simply does not appear, and the board refreshes on a schedule rather than continuously — the collection time under the table is the honest one. Distances are straight-line from the middle of the harbour.

The obvious next step is receiving AIS ourselves. It is broadcast in the clear, and a hundred-dollar software radio on this roof would hear the whole harbour without depending on anyone else's network. That is the kind of thing this building is for.
