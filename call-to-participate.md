## Call to Participate

There is a room at 4 Breadalbane Street in Hamilton that has been a mechanics
shop since about 1927, and we are not going to change it. One open room.
Concrete floor, garage height, a bay door you can drive a vehicle through.
Benches and stools along one wall for hardware, prototyping and testing;
blackboards and whiteboards along the other. A pool table at the back with a
cap that drops over it when the table is needed for something larger than a
game. That is the whole building, and it is deliberately the whole plan. What
happens in it depends entirely on who decides to show up.

The idea is not new, and we are not pretending it is. Around 1800, George
Birkbeck started giving scientific instruction to working mechanics in
Glasgow. By 1823 that had become the Glasgow Mechanics' Institution, and the
model spread quickly enough that the Hamilton and Gore Mechanics' Institute
was founded here in 1839, operating at first out of rented rooms. The premise
then was that people who work with their hands are entitled to the theory
behind the work, that the instruction is best delivered by peers rather than
sold down from a height, and that a shared room with tools and a library
beats ten people each struggling alone. None of that has been disproved. It
has mostly just been forgotten in places that used to know it well.

Hamilton is a strange and good place to remember it. This is still a working
industrial city — a port that moves steel and ore and grain, rail yards, mills
that have outlasted every prediction of their closing. The industrial capacity
is real and it is still here. What has thinned out is the connective tissue:
the places where somebody who fixes machines for a living can learn
microcontrollers, where somebody who writes software can put their hands on a
bench and find out what it costs to make a physical thing, where a person with
a product idea can meet the three people who know how to build it. Those
people all live here already. They mostly do not have a room in common.

So the room is the offer, and the specifics are ordinary on purpose: work
tables and fixed benches, assembly zones for projects that need to stay
standing between sessions, a tool room, storage bins so a project does not
have to travel home with you, and boards meant to be written on and left up.
Alongside that, training with certifications that carry weight on a résumé —
IPC and J-STD electronics standards, Linux systems, microcontrollers — taught
in partnership with certified instructors rather than improvised. Anyone can
rent the space or the room for an event. Members get the rest, and members set
the direction.

Here is the honest part. It is not open yet. The corporation is formed, the
building is identified, the first courses and partnerships are lined up, and
the membership is being raised now, before the doors open, because that is
what determines whether they open at all. This is not a finished institution
looking for customers; it is a founding cohort deciding what a hardware hall
in this city should be, at the stage where those decisions are still genuinely
open. Some of what is written on this site will turn out to be wrong, and the
people who join early are the ones who will correct it.

If any of that sounds like your kind of problem, there are several honest ways
in. Join the mailing list and watch — that costs nothing and commits you to
nothing. Take a membership at Bench, Shop or Keyholder and start using the
room from the day it opens. Take a Founder membership if you want your name
permanently attached to the group that made it possible. If you run a business
that depends on skilled technical people in this region, the Patron and
Champion tiers exist so you can sponsor equipment, a workshop series, or a
zone, and have that recognised. And if you would teach something, or want a
course that is not yet on the calendar, tell us — the training schedule is
built from what members actually ask for. Write to
patonhall.canada@gmail.com. The room is here. It has been here since 1927. The
only open question is who is going to be in it.
